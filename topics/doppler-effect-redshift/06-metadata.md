## Description
```
Why does a siren's note drop the moment it passes you? That tiny drop holds the clue to one of the biggest discoveries ever made: the universe is expanding.

This animated documentary explains the Doppler effect step by step. You'll see waves bunch up in front of a moving source and stretch out behind it, like ripples from a swimming bug on a pond. Then the story moves to light. Squeezed light shifts toward blue and stretched light shifts toward red, which is a redshift. You'll meet Christian Doppler, who first described the effect in 1842 in a paper named after the colored light of stars. Next comes the 1845 train experiment with horn players near Utrecht. Then Vesto Slipher's measurements of the spiral nebulae, and Edwin Hubble's 1929 chart showing that the farther a galaxy is, the faster it moves away. The video ends with a twist: far galaxies aren't really racing through space. Space itself is stretching.

A note on credit: Georges Lemaître proposed the expanding universe in 1927, and most of the speeds on Hubble's chart came from Slipher's measurements. In 2018, members of the International Astronomical Union voted to recommend the name "Hubble–Lemaître law."
```
