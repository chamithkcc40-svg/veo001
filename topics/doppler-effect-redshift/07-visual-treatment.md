# 07 - Visual treatment (animated cartoon, drawn entirely in code, 16:9)

Format: 1920x1080, YouTube, about 3:17. Everything is vector shapes, gradients and code-driven motion (no generated images). The reference clips supplied with Prompt B override the art direction below. The scene content, the beats and the info-visual data stay as written.

## 1. Visual concept
**"Every wave is a ring."** One visual idea runs through the whole film: the expanding ring. A siren's sound is drawn as rings. A bug on a pond makes rings. Light from a star is drawn as rings that carry colour. And in the end the rings become the universe itself, with galaxies riding on a stretching sheet. The viewer learns to read ring spacing once (squeezed means high and blue, stretched means low and red) and then sees the same picture scale up from a street to the cosmos. Ring spacing is always the storyteller. Colour and pitch follow it.

## 2. Art-direction proposal
- **Mood:** warm curiosity on Earth, quiet awe in space. Friendly, never spooky.
- **Shape language:** round and soft for the friendly world (Pip, the bug, the pond, the rings). Tall rectangles and rounded boxes for buildings, the ambulance and telescopes. Spirals and ellipses for galaxies. No sharp spikes except the siren's light flash.
- **Line:** an even 4 px dark-ink outline (#1E2233) on characters and props. Rings are drawn without outlines, as glowing strokes 3-6 px wide.
- **Shading:** flat fills with one cel-shadow tone at 15% darker. Space scenes add soft radial glows (additive blend).
- **Lighting:** the Earth scenes take place at golden late afternoon (long soft shadows to the right). Doppler's Prague study is candle-warm. Space is deep navy with self-lit galaxies.
- **Texture:** a subtle paper grain overlay (2-3% opacity, static seeded noise) on Earth scenes. A fine star dust field in space (seeded and static, with slow parallax only).
- **Palette (proposal):**
  | Role | Hex |
  |---|---|
  | Ink / outlines | #1E2233 |
  | Day sky | #F6E7C8 |
  | Street / pavement | #D9C9A8 |
  | Pond water | #6FB7C9 |
  | Squeezed / approaching / BLUE accent | #3D7BFF |
  | Stretched / receding / RED accent | #FF4A3D |
  | Neutral wave (stationary) | #FFFFFF at 80% |
  | Night space | #0B1026 |
  | Galaxy core glow | #FFF4D6 |
  | Ambulance body | #FFFFFF, stripe #FF4A3D |
- **Type:** one rounded display face for titles (for example a heavy rounded grotesk, ALL CAPS, slight positive tracking) and one clean humanist sans for captions and labels. Two families at most.
- **Banned looks:** centered text on a plain gradient; purple-blue "AI" gradients; floating filler particles; clip-art icons; real photos; logos of real emergency services (the ambulance is generic, with no real-world livery or emblem); copying any reference clip's characters or compositions.

## 4. Recurring props and motifs
- **The Rings**: the core motif. Concentric strokes that spread at a constant speed. Stationary source: even spacing, white. Moving source: tighter and BLUE ahead, wider and RED behind. The ring speed never changes, only the spacing (this matches "The waves never speed up").
- **The Pitch Line**: a small wavy line in a speech-bubble shape above Pip's ear. Tight, fast wiggles for high pitch, slow wide wiggles for low pitch. It shows sound for viewers watching muted.
- **The Barcode**: a horizontal rainbow strip (violet to red) with 4 dark vertical lines. A ghost "LAB" copy sits above it. When the source recedes, the lower strip's lines slide toward red. When it approaches, they slide toward blue.
- **The Glowing Galaxy** (SOURCE NOTES): a soft spiral with a bright cream core and two arms. Its outer glow tints BLUE when it approaches and RED when it recedes. **Label it as a picture of the shift, not what the eye sees.** The first time the tint appears, show a tiny caption "colours exaggerated" bottom-right.
- **Raisin Dough**: a beige rounded dough slab with dark raisins (galaxies) that rises in the final twist.
- **The "Twist" sting**: a quick 90° page-curl wipe with a small spiral icon, used at both "twist" lines.

## 5. Environments
1. **Sunset Street** (cold open, Ch1, close): a wide 16:9 two-point street running left to right, rowhouses, a lamp post, Pip on the sidewalk at screen-left third. The sky is #F6E7C8 fading to peach.
2. **Inside the Cab** (Ch1): a cosy dashboard, Nell's hands, a windshield showing the street rushing past.
3. **The Pond** (Ch2): a top-down view of a round pond with lily pads at the edges and reed tips at the frame border. The water is #6FB7C9 with soft caustic stripes.
4. **Prague Study, 1842** (Ch2): candlelit attic desk, papers, a window with a starry sky and Prague-style rooftops (generic spires, no specific landmark required).
5. **Dutch Railway, 1845** (Ch2): flat green fields, a steam locomotive pulling one open flatcar with horn players, and observers by the track.
6. **Light Lab** (Ch3): an abstract dark-grey studio with a floating rainbow strip and the barcode.
7. **Observatory Domes** (Ch3/Ch4): a pine-hill dome at night (Lowell-style, generic), and a larger dome with a giant telescope (Mount Wilson-style, generic). Neither has to be architecturally exact.
8. **Deep Space** (Ch3/Ch4): navy field, seeded stars, galaxies.
9. **Chart Room** (Ch4): a chalkboard-green graph board with chalk-white axes.
10. **Kitchen Counter / Cosmic Dough** (Ch4): a dough slab on a warm counter that dissolves into space.

## 6. Scene list by chapter (what is pictured for each sentence or beat)
Camera terms: WS = wide shot, MS = medium, CU = close-up, TD = top-down, INS = insert.

### Cold open - "Listen." (mood: hushed, intrigued -> awed; colour mood: warm dusk with a blue/red accent flash)
1. "Listen." **Black screen.** A single white ring pulses out from the center, then a second one. Pip's big ear fades in, filling the left of frame (CU).
2. "A siren races toward you." WS Sunset Street. A generic ambulance enters frame-right and races left. Its rings bunch up BLUE in front, toward Pip. Light bar flashes. Pitch Line above Pip: tight wiggles.
3. "The moment it passes, its note drops." The ambulance crosses Pip. The rings behind it stretch RED. The Pitch Line goes from tight to wide wiggles with a visible downward step. Pip's mouth goes "ooo".
4. "That tiny drop holds the clue..." Freeze on the stretched red rings. The camera pulls back and up FAST (whip-tilt into the sky). The street shrinks, the rings keep spreading, and they turn into a starfield.
5. "...that told us the whole universe is growing." Deep Space. A field of glowing galaxies drifts apart slightly. One hero Glowing Galaxy center-right glows and tints red at its edges. Title card: **ONE SIREN. ONE UNIVERSE.** (small, bottom-left, then fades).

### Chapter 1 - One Siren, Two Sounds (mood: warm, playful -> curious; colour: golden afternoon)
Chapter title card: "1 · ONE SIREN, TWO SOUNDS" on a street sign that swings into frame.
6. "Picture the ambulance driver." Cut to Inside the Cab (MS). Nell drives calmly.
7. "To her, the siren plays one steady note." INS: the siren on the roof emits perfectly even WHITE rings around the cab. Inside, a flat steady Pitch Line hovers by Nell's ear. She nods.
8. "But you, on the sidewalk, hear it high as it comes, and low as it goes." **Split screen, 16:9 side-by-side** (the hero comparison): LEFT = Nell's cab with an even white Pitch Line. RIGHT = Pip on the sidewalk, Pitch Line tight-BLUE, then wide-RED as the ambulance passes.
9. "Same siren. Two sounds." Two big labels pop in one at a time, one per half: "SAME SIREN" (center divider), then "TWO SOUNDS".
10. "And you're both right." Nell and Pip both give a thumbs-up across the divider. The divider wipes away.
11. "So what changed? Not the siren." The siren on the roof gets a big question mark, then a playful red X "NOT THE SIREN".
12. "Something in the air between you." The camera drops low (ground-level WS). The air between the cab and Pip gets a shimmering highlight. The rings appear as faint lines in the air, which leads to the next chapter.

### Chapter 2 - Ripples (mood: calm, clear -> lively -> confident -> gentle storytelling -> brightening -> intrigued; colour: cool pond teal, then candle amber, then spring green)
Chapter title card: "2 · RIPPLES", the letters drawn as a ripple ring.
13. "Sound travels in waves, like ripples on a pond." Match-cut: the air rings become water rings. TD The Pond, a slow push-in.
14. "Picture a bug sitting still, tapping the water." Skipper lands center-pond (TD), taps with one leg. Each tap = one ring.
15. "Perfect rings spread out evenly." **RIPPLE ANIMATION A (stationary):** concentric white rings, equal spacing, spreading at a constant speed, fading at the edges. Hold ~2 s so the pattern can be read.
16. "Now the bug swims forward while it taps." Skipper swims to the right at a steady speed, scarf fluttering, still tapping at the same rhythm.
17. "The rings in front get squeezed together." **RIPPLE ANIMATION B (moving source):** each new ring's center is where Skipper was at that tap, so the rings pile up ahead (right side) and turn BLUE. A small bracket shows the spacing narrowing.
18. "The rings behind get stretched apart." The left-side spacing widens and turns RED. A second bracket shows the wider spacing. Skipper looks back over its shoulder.
19. "Sound does the same." Split, side by side: the pond rings on the left, the street ambulance rings on the right, identical patterns overlaid.
20. "Squeezed waves reach your ear more often, so the pitch sounds higher." INS: Pip's ear at frame-right. Blue rings hit it in a rapid rhythm; each hit flashes a small dot on a "hits per second" tick bar. The Pitch Line is tight.
21. "Stretched waves arrive less often, so it sounds lower." Red rings hit slower and the tick bar spaces out. The Pitch Line goes wide.
22. "The waves never speed up. Only their spacing changes." Diagram shot: two rings race side by side across the frame (a blue one and a red one), each with a small speedometer showing the SAME needle position. Then the spacing brackets glow. Caption: "SAME SPEED · DIFFERENT SPACING".
23. "In eighteen forty-two, a frail math professor in Prague worked this out." Transition: a ripple dissolves into candlelight. Prague Study (MS), Doppler writing at his desk, coughing softly. **Timeline ribbon** slides in at the bottom with a marker at "1842". Label: "Christian Doppler · Prague".
24. "His name was Christian Doppler, and he had once nearly quit and moved to America." Short memory beat in sepia tint: Doppler closes a suitcase, a ship silhouette in the window. He sets the suitcase down. Back to colour.
25. "Three years later, a Dutch scientist put horn players on an open train car." WS Dutch Railway: the locomotive pulls the open car from left to right, and the three horn players blow. Timeline marker "1845". Label: "Buys Ballot · near Utrecht".
26. "People beside the track heard the notes bend." The observers cup their ears; Pitch Lines above them go tight, then wide as the car passes. Rings blue ahead, red behind (the same visual language as the ambulance).
27. "But here's the twist." The Twist sting (page curl with a spiral icon). The music drops out.
28. "Doppler didn't name his paper after sound. He named it after the colored light of stars." Back in the study: a close-up on the paper's title page with a period-style heading. The on-screen paraphrase reads: "On the coloured light of the double stars..." Doppler looks up at the window, where two stars twinkle, one bluish and one reddish. The camera pushes through the window into the stars.

### Chapter 3 - Light Has a Siren Too (mood: soft intrigue -> clear -> wry -> proud -> curious -> storytelling -> tense; colour: dark studio with a rainbow accent, then night navy)
Chapter title card: "3 · LIGHT HAS A SIREN TOO" in a glowing neon-tube style.
29. "Light is a wave too, and for light, spacing means color." Light Lab. A white ring pulses out from a small star. The ring is drawn as a wavy line, and as the wiggle spacing is changed the stroke colour changes along the rainbow.
30. "Squeeze the waves, and light shifts toward blue." The star moves toward the camera-right viewer; its front rings tighten and turn BLUE.
31. "Stretch them, and it shifts toward red." It moves away; the rings behind it stretch and turn RED.
32. "That's a redshift." **The Glowing Galaxy hero moment (SOURCE NOTES):** the star becomes the Glowing Galaxy, centered. Its glow moves smoothly from BLUE (approaching) through cream to RED (receding) as the galaxy drifts slightly backward in depth (it scales down by about 10%). The word **REDSHIFT** forms from red rings. Small caption bottom-right: "colours exaggerated".
33. "Doppler guessed wrong about star colors. The shift was far too small to notice." Back in the study: the two window stars are shown at TRUE scale. They barely change, and a magnifier icon shows "too small". Doppler gives a small sheepish shrug (wry, gentle).
34. "But he predicted astronomers would one day use it to measure how stars move." Doppler writes; his quill trail becomes a dotted arrow that flies off into the night sky toward a telescope icon.
35. "They did." A stamp-style checkmark lands. Quick.
36. "Starlight carries a barcode of dark lines, set by its atoms." The Barcode prop: a rainbow strip with 4 dark lines. Tiny atom icons sit above each line, linked to it by a thin tether. A ghost "LAB" strip sits above for reference.
37. "If the barcode slides toward red, the source is moving away." The lower strip's lines slide right (toward red), and the offset is measured with an arrow against the LAB strip. A tiny galaxy drifts away beside it.
38. "In the nineteen tens, Vesto Slipher read those barcodes in faint spirals in the sky." Observatory dome at night on a pine hill (WS), then MS of Slipher at the eyepiece holding a glass plate. Timeline: "1912-1914". Label: "Vesto Slipher · Lowell Observatory".
39. "Andromeda was coming toward us, about three hundred kilometers every second." The Andromeda spiral (a large tilted ellipse) glides toward the camera. Its barcode lines slide toward BLUE, and its glow tints blue. **Info label:** "ANDROMEDA · approaching · ~300 km/s" (source: Slipher 1913 per Astronomy.com).
40. "But nearly all the others were rushing away." Pull back: a scatter of about 15 small spirals (by 1914, Slipher had results for 15) on a sky map. All but a couple tint RED and drift outward, each with a tiny red barcode tag. Label: "~15 spirals by 1914 · nearly all receding".
41. "Why would almost everything flee from us?" The small Milky Way marker (labelled "US") sits in the middle with the red galaxies fleeing around it. Pip appears in a corner looking up, puzzled. The music holds a suspended chord.

### Chapter 4 - The Running Universe (mood: dramatic -> building -> awed peak -> fair -> hushed -> awed -> warm; colour: navy into chalkboard green, peak gold, then warm kitchen into cosmic navy)
Chapter title card: "4 · THE RUNNING UNIVERSE", the letters spreading apart as they appear.
42. "Enter Edwin Hubble." Low-angle WS: the giant telescope in its dome, the dome slit opening. Hubble steps into a pool of light. Label: "Edwin Hubble · Mount Wilson".
43. "In nineteen twenty-three, with the most powerful telescope of its day, he proved Andromeda was a whole galaxy, far beyond our own." Glass-plate INS: a star in Andromeda blinks bright-dim-bright, and Hubble marks it. Then a WS with a scale bar: the Milky Way disk on the left and Andromeda pushed far to the right, the gap visibly widening. Timeline: "1923".
44. "Next, he measured how far away other galaxies were." A row of galaxies lines up, each with a measuring-tape distance tag that extends out to it (no specific numbers).
45. "In nineteen twenty-nine, he set his distances beside their speeds, most of them Slipher's." Chart Room. Two stacks of cards slide together: "HUBBLE: DISTANCES" and "SLIPHER: SPEEDS" (most of them). Timeline: "1929".
46. "A pattern jumped out." **INFO-VISUAL - Hubble 1929 diagram (schematic):** chalk axes: x = "distance", y = "speed away". 24 dots appear one by one (Hubble used 24 galaxies, PNAS 2015). A rising straight line draws itself through the cloud. Small credit label: "after Hubble 1929, PNAS · 24 galaxies · schematic". Dot positions are illustrative unless Prompt B digitizes them from the 1929 paper's figure. Do not invent axis numbers. The only allowed number tag is a note near the top-right group: "Virgo galaxies ~1,000 km/s" (PNAS 2015).
47. "The farther a galaxy is, the faster it moves away." Each dot becomes a tiny galaxy with a red arrow whose length grows with distance.
48. "Not just from us. From each other." **Grid demonstration:** a 5x3 grid of galaxies on a sheet. The sheet scales up evenly. Highlight any one galaxy (ring it in gold): the arrows from ITS point of view also point away. Repeat for a second galaxy.
49. "The universe is expanding." The peak: the camera pulls back fast through the galaxy field. Every galaxy's glow tints red with distance (near ones cream, far ones deep red; colours exaggerated). Gold title text: **THE UNIVERSE IS EXPANDING**.
50. "A Belgian priest, Georges Lemaître, had proposed this two years earlier." Side panel slides in (16:9 left third): Lemaître writing pages; timeline marker "1927" sits before "1929". Label: "Georges Lemaître · 1927".
51. "But Hubble's chart is what convinced astronomers." The chart on the right; a small crowd of silhouetted astronomers nod in front of it; the 1927 and 1929 markers both glow (fair framing: both lit).
52. "And one last twist." Twist sting (the same page curl). Music drops to a soft pad.
53. "Far galaxies aren't really speeding through space like sirens down a street." A quick recall: a tiny ambulance zooms across a starry road, then a big gentle "X" over it.
54. "Space itself is stretching, like rising dough pulling raisins apart." Kitchen Counter: the raisin dough rises in a smooth timelapse. The raisins (drawn as mini galaxies) spread apart without moving through the dough. Then the dough dissolves into a navy space sheet with the same raisins as galaxies.
55. "Their light stretches with it, toward red." **The Glowing Galaxy finale + stretching wave:** one long wavy light ray travels from a far galaxy to a tiny Milky Way. As the sheet stretches, the wave's wiggles widen and its colour shifts blue -> cream -> RED along the path. The galaxy's own glow follows. Caption: "colours exaggerated".

### Close (mood: warm, reflective; colour: back to golden street, then night sky)
56. "So the next time a siren drops its note as it passes, listen." Back on Sunset Street, now at dusk. Pip hears an ambulance pass; the rings go blue then red, and the Pitch Line drops. Pip smiles knowingly.
57. "You're hearing the same clue that told us the universe is growing." Pip looks up. The red rings drift into the sky and become galaxies. Slow crane-up to the night sky. A final hold on the starfield with end-screen space (see 06-metadata.md). Final text, small: **LISTEN FOR THE DROP**.

## 8. On-screen text plan
- **Captions:** burned-in narration captions, always on (many viewers watch muted). Use 1-2 lines max, bottom-center, inside the 16:9 title-safe area (keep 5% margins), sentence by sentence, matching 03-script.md word for word. The words "BLUE" or "RED" in captions may take their accent colours.
- **Chapter title cards:** 4 cards (see each chapter), on screen for about 2 s, each drawn inside its world (street sign, ripple, neon tube, spreading letters).

## 10. Transition ideas
- **Ring match-cuts** (the signature): a ring in one world becomes a ring in the next (air rings to pond rings, ripple to candle-glow, stretched siren rings to starfield, red rings to galaxies at the end).
- **Twist sting:** a page curl with a spiral icon, used exactly twice.
- **Whip-tilt** from the street to the sky (cold open), mirrored as a crane-up at the close.
- **Push-through-window** from Doppler's study into the stars (Ch2 to Ch3).
- **Dough-to-space dissolve** (Ch4).
- **Hard cuts** for the comedic Ch1 beats.

## 11. Shot variety
WS establishing shots (street, railway, domes, galaxy field). MS characters (Nell, Doppler, Slipher, Hubble). CU (Pip's ear, the paper title). Top-down (pond). INS (siren light, glass plate, barcode, speedometers). Split-screen comparisons (driver vs. listener, pond vs. street). Data shots (timeline, Hubble diagram, spiral map). Kinetic type (REDSHIFT, THE UNIVERSE IS EXPANDING). Never run the same shot type more than twice in a row. Something moves on screen at least every 2-3 s (rings are always spreading somewhere).

## 12. Aspect-aware notes (16:9)
- Motion runs left to right across the wide frame (the ambulance, Skipper, the train), so the squeezed (blue) side and stretched (red) side read clearly at the left and right of frame.
- Use side-by-side comparisons as the main teaching device (driver | listener; pond | street; LAB barcode over the observed barcode; 1927 | 1929).
- Keep captions in the bottom 18% and labels in the lower-left third so the center stays free for the wave action.
- Thumbnail-friendly frame: the split of "ambulance with blue/red rings | glowing galaxy blue-to-red" (Cold open beat 4-5) doubles as Thumbnail 1.
- Leave the upper-left and upper-right sky clear in the last 12 s for end-screen elements.

## 13. Accuracy guardrails for the animator
- Rings must spread at the same speed in every direction from the point where they were made. Only the source moves. Never draw faster rings in front.
- The source must stay slower than the rings (no shock cone).
- The driver's rings, as heard inside the cab, stay even.
- Real galaxies don't visibly change colour to the eye. Every tint is a diagram, so keep the "colours exaggerated" tag on.
- The expansion grid scales evenly, and every galaxy sees the others receding.
- In the dough, the raisins don't swim. Only the dough grows.
