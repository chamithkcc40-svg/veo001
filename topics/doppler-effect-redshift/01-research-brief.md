## Key facts table

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | A siren sounds higher as it approaches and drops in pitch as it passes and recedes. | S2, S3 |
| F2 | The waves reach the ear closer together as the source approaches and farther apart as it recedes. | S2, S1 |
| F3 | Ahead of a moving source the waves bunch up (shorter wavelength, higher frequency, higher pitch); behind it they spread out (longer wavelength, lower pitch). | S1 |
| F4 | The sound itself always travels at the speed of sound; the source's motion changes the spacing, not the wave speed. | S1 |
| F5 | Christian Doppler presented the idea on 25 May 1842 in Prague, in a paper titled "On the coloured light of the double stars and certain other stars of the heavens". | S4, S3 |
| F6 | Doppler's health was frail; before getting his Prague post he had been close to emigrating to America. | S4 |
| F7 | His specific example (star colours of double stars) was wrong in practice: the effect is far too small to change colours visibly. He predicted the principle would one day let astronomers measure star motions. | S4 |
| F8 | In 1845 Buys Ballot tested it in the Netherlands with brass horn players on an open train car on a new line near Utrecht; observers by the track noted the change in tone. | S5, S6, S4 |
| F9 | Light behaves like a wave and gets a Doppler-like shift: receding = longer, redder wavelengths ("redshift"); approaching = toward violet/blue. | S2, S3 |
| F10 | Light is about a million times faster than sound, so we do not notice its shift in daily life. | S2 |
| F11 | Astronomers measure redshift by comparing a galaxy's spectral lines with known laboratory line positions. | S2 |
| F12 | Slipher (Lowell Observatory) measured Andromeda approaching at about 300 km/s (1912-13). | S7 |
| F13 | By 1914 Slipher had results for 15 spiral nebulae; nearly all were receding. | S7 |
| F14 | In 1923 Hubble, with the 100-inch Hooker telescope at Mount Wilson, found a Cepheid in Andromeda proving it lies beyond the Milky Way. | S11 |
| F15 | Hubble's 1929 paper used 24 galaxies; velocities mostly Slipher's, distances Hubble's own, measured from the brightness of their stars. | S8 |
| F16 | Result: the farther a galaxy, the faster it recedes (roughly linear). | S8, S10 |
| F17 | Hubble's distances were too small by about seven times; his rate (500 km/s/Mpc) vs today's ~70; the basic relation still stands. | S8 |
| F18 | Hubble wrote to Slipher (1953) of "your velocities and my distances". | S7 (quote attested in S7; also many academic secondary refs) |
| F19 | Lemaître published an expanding-universe solution with a velocity-distance hint in 1927, in French in a Belgian journal, largely unnoticed. | S8, S13 |
| F20 | In 2018 IAU members voted (78% in favour) to recommend the name "Hubble-Lemaître law". | S12 |
| F21 | Cosmological redshift is, strictly, not a Doppler shift through space but a stretching of light by the expansion of space itself; ESA uses a rising raisin-bread analogy. | S2, S10 |
| F22 | NASA: as space expands, light traveling across it elongates and becomes redder; farther galaxies show more redshift. | S10 |
| F23 | Hubble himself in 1929 did not connect his result to expanding-universe solutions; others did shortly after. | S8, S13 |

## The angle
One sound, heard on any street, holds the key that told us the universe is growing. The video follows the idea's round trip: born in the stars (Doppler's 1842 paper was about star colours), proven on a train (1845), and carried back to the stars, where it turned the universe from a still room into a rising loaf.

## The audience
Curious general YouTube viewers, 14+, no physics background; they know what a siren sounds like and that "the universe is expanding", but not how anyone could know that.

## The emotional core
Wonder from the ordinary: the same "neeee-yowww" you hear on your street is the reason we know the cosmos is growing. Secondary stake: a frail, overlooked teacher's odd idea, wrong in its first example, turns out to be right about everything that matters.

## The central question / curiosity gap
"How can a sound on your street tell us the universe is getting bigger?"

## One-sentence shareable idea
The pitch drop of a passing siren and the reddening light of far galaxies are the same trick of waves, and it is how we found out the universe is expanding.
Sharing this makes me look: smart and curious.

## Fact-policy notes on the user's IDEA
- "Hubble used the Doppler effect to discover the universe is expanding": SOURCES CONFLICT on credit and interpretation. NASA (S10) and ESA (S2) say Hubble discovered/confirmed it in 1929; PNAS (S8) calls his paper the definitive evidence; Science (S13) and IAU (S12) record that Lemaître proposed it first, and S8/S13 note Hubble's velocities were mostly Slipher's and Hubble did not himself frame it as expansion in 1929. Per policy, the claim is NOT changed; the script keeps Hubble central and adds sourced context (Slipher's velocities, Lemaître's earlier proposal). Logged in handoff.md and 04-fact-check.md.
- "Redshift of light ... Doppler effect": essentially right as a teaching step (S2, S3), but ESA (S2) adds that distant-galaxy redshift is strictly from expanding space. Not a correction of the user's claim; added as the video's twist, sourced.
